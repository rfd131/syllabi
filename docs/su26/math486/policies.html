Use of Generative AI

Submitting work produced by generative AI as a substitute for work that represents your own effort and understanding is a clear violation of academic integrity. This applies to ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot, and any similar tools.

At the same time, it's important to recognize that generative AI has become a powerful tool for learning and it can be used appropriately.

Appropriate use of generative AI

Large language models may make errors and lead you astray but advanced large language models can now handle essentially any content from the undergraduate math curriculum, game theory included. Advanced models can help you hone in on specific details where you need more clarity and engage in an extended back-and-forth exploring examples and edge cases. These are real mathematical skills and AI can be effective for this kind of exploration.

Some examples of prompts that can support your learning: What is the difference between perfect and imperfect information in an extensive form game?, How is imperfect information represented in a game tree?, What is a mixed strategy, and when are they used?, or Walk me through a worked example of finding a subgame perfect equilibrium. Conversations of this kind are appropriate.

Inappropriate use of generative AI

The benefit of this course comes from your effort in working through problems, developing problem solving skills, and making the important concepts and applications from the course your own. AI cannot do that work for you.

A note on what's changed: In past versions of this statement students were warned about AI producing confident-sounding errors. This is less of a concern with current models than it was even a year ago, but a related issue remains: it is easy to read an AI explanation, or an instructor's solution for that matter, and feel that you understand it and discover later that you cannot reproduce the reasoning yourself. There are multiple ways to check your understanding: Can you work through key problems on your own, and not just follow along through a provided explanation? Could you explain your reasoning to someone else? Can you reason about how small changes would impact a definition, theorem, or result? Keep these skills in mind as you consider your use of AI.
